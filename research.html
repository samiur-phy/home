Research

I look for places where a beyond-Standard-Model hypothesis leaves a signature that data already in hand can test—then work out how tightly that data constrains it.

Heavy neutral leptons from the Sun

A tau-philic heavy neutral lepton produced in the solar core would decay in flight and deposit electrons and photons that space-based instruments have been recording for decades. I use quiet-time electron spectra from SOHO/EPHIN and Ulysses to set exclusion limits in the mass–mixing plane, treating the measured flux itself as the background rather than assuming a background model.

Cosmogenic neutrinos and Lorentz invariance violation

Neutrinos produced when ultra-high-energy cosmic rays interact with background photons travel cosmological baselines, which makes their arriving flavor composition an unusually sensitive probe of new physics. I model these fluxes and ask how much Lorentz-violating operators of different mass dimension can distort the flavor triangle before next-generation observatories would resolve the difference.

Neutrino mixing and tau neutrino signatures

Two experiments have ever detected tau neutrinos in a laboratory, which leaves the tau sector the least constrained corner of the flavor structure. I study how mixing parameters evolve under renormalization group flow and what that implies for observables such as double-bang topologies at IceCube.
